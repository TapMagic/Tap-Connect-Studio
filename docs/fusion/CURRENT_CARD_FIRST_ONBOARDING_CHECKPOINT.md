# TapConnect Card-First Onboarding Checkpoint

Source commit: `fd46ad42d7b6bc95160d1e3bc901707d5129387a`

## Locked Product Center

The Card is the center of TapConnect.

TapConnect is the living Card.

TapConnect Studio is the system working around the Card to:

- create it
- connect customers to it
- retain the relationship
- operate the relationship
- prove what the Card accomplished

Onboarding must not merely create an account or send the Owner into Settings.

Onboarding must understand the business, establish a useful Brand foundation, and generate a valuable first living Card.

Locked onboarding promise:

TELL US ABOUT YOUR BUSINESS  
→ WE FOUND YOUR BRAND  
→ HERE IS YOUR FIRST CARD

Do not land a new Owner in an empty editor.

## Target Owner Experience

The new Owner should experience a guided, progressive journey:

1. Tell TapConnect about the business.
2. TapConnect gathers and organizes Business Knowledge.
3. TapConnect discovers and prepares Brand identity.
4. The Owner reviews uncertainty and approves important facts and Brand choices.
5. TapConnect generates a useful first Card draft automatically.
6. The Owner sees and edits the Card early.
7. The Owner previews it as a customer.
8. The Owner clearly understands what is saved, draft, unpublished, or published.
9. The Owner prepares the first Tap Point or distribution path.
10. The Owner begins with a useful system, not a blank canvas.

The Owner should not need to understand TapConnect’s internal product architecture before receiving value.

## Onboarding Inputs

Begin with the smallest useful set of inputs:

- business name
- website or domain
- business category
- location
- contact information
- primary products or services
- social links where available
- existing logo or imagery where available
- first desired customer outcome

Possible first outcomes include:

- collect and retain customer contacts
- get more reviews
- promote an offer
- fill appointments
- provide directions or contact actions
- launch loyalty
- answer customer questions
- create a useful general business Card

Ask about the desired outcome early.

Do not begin with a long administrative settings questionnaire.

Use progressive disclosure.

## Business Knowledge

TapConnect may use approved sources such as:

- Owner input
- website/domain
- uploaded files
- approved social links
- existing Brand assets
- connected systems where available

Business Knowledge must preserve:

- source
- provenance
- confidence
- contradictions
- missing information
- Owner approval status
- last verified time

The system must never invent:

- prices
- offers
- policies
- legal terms
- consent
- business facts
- customer records
- opening hours
- services
- claims

Ask one concise batch of meaningful questions rather than interrupting the Owner repeatedly.

Use plain language.

## Brand Starter Kit

Prepare a Brand Starter Kit using available approved inputs and provider capabilities.

Potential sources include:

- website findings
- uploaded assets
- existing Studio assets
- Logo.dev
- Pexels
- Brand colors inferred from approved logos or pages
- professional font recommendations

The Brand Starter Kit may include:

- primary logo
- alternate mark
- primary colors
- supporting colors
- font recommendations
- imagery direction
- visual style direction
- voice cues
- accessibility findings
- source and rights metadata

Owner decisions should include:

- Keep
- Replace
- Compare
- Edit
- Approve
- Lock
- Ignore
- Use only here
- Use across Brand
- Needs rights confirmation

Do not silently override approved or locked Brand choices.

Do not claim a Brand asset is approved merely because it was discovered.

## First Card Generation

Generate a complete first Card draft from:

- approved Business Knowledge
- approved Brand choices
- selected first outcome
- business category
- available contact and action data
- safe industry-aware defaults

The Owner must not face a blank Card.

The first Card should include sensible content such as:

- recognizable business identity
- approved logo
- business name
- clear primary message
- Save Contact where appropriate
- Website
- Directions
- Phone or Email where appropriate
- selected primary outcome
- relevant secondary actions
- TapSave entry where appropriate
- accessible text and contrast
- safe responsive behavior

Do not insert unavailable or invented actions.

Provider-dependent actions must display honest readiness states.

The generated Card must use the existing Creative Studio, shared Brand system, media system, typography system, preview system, and Card renderer.

Do not create another Card builder.

## Owner Review and Approval

The Owner should be able to:

- keep the prepared Card
- edit directly
- compare useful alternatives
- replace discovered assets
- approve Business Knowledge
- approve Brand choices
- Reset to Brand
- create a Card-only override
- lock important Brand choices
- see why TapConnect recommended something
- see what remains uncertain

Use clear status language:

- Saved draft
- Unsaved changes
- Draft changes not published
- Preview as customer
- Publish changes
- Open published Card

Do not use ambiguous wording such as Done, Live, Active, or Return without explaining the result.

## Autopilot Role

Autopilot is not merely a copy generator.

For onboarding, Autopilot should:

- gather approved source material
- extract facts with provenance
- detect contradictions
- identify missing information
- ask one concise question batch
- recommend the first customer outcome
- prepare the Brand Starter Kit
- generate the first Card draft
- validate required fields
- simulate representative customer paths
- identify readiness problems
- preserve approved content during regeneration
- explain recommendations plainly

Autopilot must not publish, send, charge, contact customers, or invent facts.

Deterministic policy—not the model—must control consequential actions.

## Card-First Journey

The intended visible vertical journey is:

Business onboarding  
→ Business Knowledge  
→ Brand Starter Kit  
→ Owner approval  
→ first TapConnect Card draft  
→ Preview as customer  
→ Saved draft  
→ publication readiness  
→ first Tap Point or distribution path  
→ permanent living Card relationship  
→ analytics readiness

The Card should become visible early and update as the Owner approves knowledge and Brand choices.

Do not hide the Card until the final onboarding screen.

## No-Shoehorn Law

The onboarding architecture must not create:

- another Card model
- another Brand system
- another media library
- another template system
- another Business Knowledge store
- another Autopilot pipeline
- another preview system
- another publishing system
- provider-specific core models
- disposable onboarding-only data that cannot become durable business state

Onboarding should populate and activate the permanent platform systems.

## Desired First Implementation Slice

After checkpoint confirmation, the first implementation slice should deliver one complete visible path:

1. Begin onboarding.
2. Enter business name and website/domain.
3. Select a first customer outcome.
4. Gather or enter core business facts.
5. Review a concise Business Knowledge summary.
6. Review a Brand Starter Kit.
7. Approve or replace logo, colors, fonts, and imagery.
8. Generate the first Card draft.
9. Show the Card immediately.
10. Preview as customer.
11. Save the draft.
12. Clearly show that it is not published.

Do not begin by building every future onboarding table or empty module.

The slice must be visible, usable, testable, responsive, accessible, and based on the final platform architecture.
